macbox vs Docker-OSX and OSX-KVM

Docker-OSX and OSX-KVM are the well-known ways to run macOS on Linux, and macbox stands on the same foundation: QEMU, KVM and OpenCore. The difference is the GPU, and what the VM is for. macbox gives macOS Metal on the host's own GPU without passthrough, and it is built around Xcode, the iOS Simulator and AI coding agents.

{{meta}}

In short

  • Pick macbox to develop and test iOS apps on a Linux machine, with Metal on an Intel or AMD GPU you keep using on the host, and with agent tooling.
  • Pick Docker-OSX or OSX-KVM for a range of macOS versions, a GPU you can pass through, or a general-purpose macOS VM.
  • Pick a Mac or a cloud service for the newest Xcode and iOS, or where Apple's license must be met.

The GPU difference

Docker-OSX and OSX-KVM give macOS an emulated display adapter. macOS has no Metal driver for it, so the desktop, Xcode and the iOS Simulator draw in software, unless you pass a physical GPU through with VFIO. Passthrough needs a card macOS has drivers for, and that card then belongs to the VM, not the host.

macbox gives QEMU Apple's paravirtual GPU device through Reims vGPU. macOS's built-in driver talks to it, and metal2vulkan runs the Metal work on the host's Vulkan driver. On an Intel Arrow Lake iGPU, that is enough for a 1x iOS Simulator to render a heavy Liquid Glass scene at 92 to 96 fps. How it works, with measurements.

The scope difference

Docker-OSX and OSX-KVM aim to boot macOS, across many versions, for whatever you need it for. macbox does less and goes further along one path: macOS 26, then Xcode, a pool of simulators tuned for a VM (mostly 1x, with background services trimmed), Metro on Linux for Expo and React Native, T3 Code's Device panel, and mac-sim and sim-fps for agents. One CLI, macbox, runs it, and macbox next always says what's left to do.

When macbox isn't the right tool

  • You need Xcode 27, iOS 27 Simulators or anything newer: Apple ships those for Apple silicon only. Use a Mac or a cloud service.
  • You need an older macOS release, or a GPU passed through for other reasons: Docker-OSX or OSX-KVM.
  • Your host has only an NVIDIA GPU: macbox's image doesn't ship NVIDIA's driver yet.
  • You need to stay within Apple's macOS license: it allows macOS only on Apple-branded hardware.
  • Commercial use: macbox's own code is under the PolyForm Noncommercial License, so commercial use needs the author's permission.
